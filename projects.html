Inequality in academia from a Network Science perspective

In this project, we have decided to focus on a preferential attachment, majority/minority network with vertex removals. We will use definitions of power inequality and glass ceiling provided by Avin et al, and check whether these emerge in our simplistic model already. This model is inspired by real world scenario of academic and citation networks, which have consistently observed inequality, particularly in STEM fields. It is essential to study these keeping in mind vertex removals, which are very prominent and vastly alter the system. We have already observed (in data from 2000 to now) that the academic collaboration network in a slowly evolving network, where number of people who join is only slightly higher than those who leave. The model parameters we will be focusing on are p (probability that a node is added at a time step), f (proportion of minority) and r (rate of removal of minority). A combination of these parameters provide interesting results in inequality analysis. We hope that some of these results can be used to derive conclusions about which parameter has to be changed to improve condition of minority in the network (for eg, increasing p is beneficial for minority even when f is low). Hopefully, these results provide some guidance on which parameters need to be controlled when implementing solutions to promote gender equality in academia.

Opinion dynamics with advantage

We consider a network, where each vertex has one of two opinions. At every time step, a new node enters the system and connects with existing vertices with preferential attachment. Suppose it connects to k nodes of opinion 1, then the node chooses opinion 1 with probability k/m. What happens when we let this process run forever? Is there convergence of any one type? This model was studied by Antunovic et al, where they proved the existence of limiting distributions which have full support on [0,1], have no atoms (in the linear case). We are currently considering a slight adaptation of this model, what if nodes of type 1 are allowed to switch to type 2 with probability p? (This is a work in progress).
